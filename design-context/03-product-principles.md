# 03 — Product Principles

These are the **non-visual** principles that govern the product: educational philosophy, trust, usability, accessibility, speed-to-value, and tone. They should constrain any redesign regardless of the visual direction chosen. (Visual/brand principles are intentionally excluded.)

## Educational philosophy (non-negotiable)

1. **Evidence-based, not intuitive.** Everything aligns with the science of reading and structured literacy. Reading aloud with a child is not enough; dyslexia requires **explicit, systematic, sequential** instruction. All content is grounded in recognized standards and research (IDA Knowledge & Practice Standards, Yale Center for Dyslexia & Creativity, National Center on Improving Literacy, What Works Clearinghouse, peer-reviewed literature).

2. **Orton-Gillingham / structured literacy where applicable.** Skills are taught in a deliberate sequence, cumulatively, with each session reviewing prior skills before introducing new ones.

3. **Multisensory by design.** Engage auditory + visual + tactile/kinesthetic pathways together (e.g., tapping sounds, manipulating tiles, tracing). This is treated as a neurological principle for strengthening sound-to-symbol mapping, not a "learning styles" fad.

4. **Short, consistent practice beats long, infrequent practice.** Sessions are **15–20 minutes maximum**, with activity changes roughly every 5 minutes. Frequent short sessions build durable neural pathways better than occasional long ones.

5. **Instructional level, not frustration level.** Practice text should let the child miss no more than about **1 word in 10**. Challenge without overwhelm.

6. **No guessing.** Discourage guessing from pictures or first letters; teach decoding and give **immediate, unambiguous corrective feedback** so errors aren't internalized.

7. **"I do, we do, you do."** The parent always models first; the child attempts with support; then independently. Modeling is never skipped.

## Trust (the core moat)

8. **Specialist-first honesty.** The product openly states that a trained specialist is the best outcome and tells parents how to find one — positioning Phono as the **bridge**, not the replacement. This counter-intuitive honesty is the single biggest trust driver.

9. **Parent, not professor.** The voice is a knowledgeable neighbor who did the research as a parent — credible *because* it mirrors the customer's own position, not because of a credential.

10. **Honesty over false hope.** No miracle claims, no "dyslexia is a superpower" oversell. Honest framing: the whole child, *and* a child who needs specific help. Show what worked, don't promise outcomes.

11. **Non-judgmental about the system.** The system is broken; teachers are trapped in it too. Don't blame parents or teachers.

## Usability & speed-to-value

12. **Start tonight.** Every tool resolves to one doable action this evening. No multi-week ramp-up, no prerequisite training for the parent.

13. **No training required.** An untrained adult must be able to deliver a tool correctly. Tools carry explicit scripts: what to say, what to do, and what to do when the child errs.

14. **Clearly labeled.** Each tool states its age/grade band, skill focus, and estimated time up front.

15. **Fits real family life.** Minimal materials, short sessions, low setup. Respect the parent's scarce time and attention.

## Accessibility (functional requirements)

16. **Built for struggling readers from the ground up**, not a generic worksheet with an accessibility mode bolted on. Reading material aimed at the child must be highly legible and easy to decode (this is an accessibility requirement; specific visual treatments are out of scope for this brief — see `07-design-constraints.md`).

17. **Reduce shame and pressure in every interaction.** Progress is shown in terms of effort and consistency (e.g., sessions completed and time spent), **not accuracy percentages or comparisons**. Error states must be gentle and never label the child as "wrong" or "failing."

18. **Celebrate small wins.** Make progress visible and frequent; recognize the child's strengths to buffer self-esteem.

## Tone

19. **Encouraging, warm, plain-spoken.** Reduce performance anxiety. Plain language without oversimplifying — give the "why," not just the steps. Specific over generic (name the exact fear, the exact technique, the exact age).
