# Phono — Design Brief

**Purpose of this document:** This is the single, self-contained brief a design studio (or another AI) needs to begin a complete rebrand and redesign of Phono. It explains the customer, the product, the business, the goals, and the constraints — **without revealing any previous visual identity**. There is no description anywhere in this folder of prior logos, colors, typography, layouts, or UI. You are free to design from a blank slate.

The companion files add depth:
`01-product-summary` · `02-customer-personas` · `03-product-principles` · `04-features` · `05-user-flows` · `06-business-context` · `07-design-constraints` · `08-open-questions`.

---

## 1. The customer

There are two users and one influencer.

- **The buyer (primary): a parent**, usually a mother (~32–45), of a child in grades K–8 who is dyslexic or visibly struggling to read. She is scared, guilt-ridden, and afraid of making things worse. She has often been told by the school to "wait and see," may be on a months-long evaluation waitlist, and can't easily afford $75–$100/session tutoring. She wants to help her child **tonight**, but doesn't feel qualified and is terrified of doing it wrong. She needs **confidence, credibility, low time cost, and reassurance**.

- **The end user: the child (K–8)**, who struggles to match letters to sounds and reads slowly and effortfully. Often a bright, creative thinker. Carries frustration, avoidance, and shame about reading. Needs **short, multisensory, low-pressure, success-rich** activities with immediate, gentle feedback — never anything that signals "you're wrong" or "you're behind."

- **The influencer: educators** (teachers, reading specialists, SLPs) who lend credibility and may share the materials. Not the buyers.

**The core tension a designer must hold:** every experience must work for an *untrained, anxious adult to deliver correctly* AND be *engaging and non-shaming for a struggling child*.

---

## 2. The product

**Phono** is the company; **Toolkits** are the product. A toolkit is a parent-led, structured-literacy reading-intervention program: **20 "tools" (activities) across 4 skill units**, run in short home sessions. Every tool tells the parent exactly what to say and do, including how to respond to errors — no training required.

Grade-banded line: **Foundation (K–2)**, **Fluency (3–5, already built)**, **Independence (6–8)**, and a cross-grade **Summer** toolkit (the near-term launch).

**How it's used:** a parent runs a **15–20 minute session** a few times a week:
Sound Play → Word Work → Reading & Fluency, with Comprehension woven in, plus daily audiobook/audio-assisted reading on the side. Progress is tracked by **consistency and effort** (sessions done, wins logged, skills checked off) — deliberately **not** by accuracy scores.

**Today the product is printable documents** (a downloadable bundle). The roadmap moves toward software: a parent-guide chatbot, an interactive session app, an AI tutoring agent, and a vetted specialist directory.

---

## 3. The business

- **Mission:** *"Stand in the gap between struggling families and trained specialists — give parents the tools to help their child right now, while connecting them to the experts who can take them further."*
- **Market:** ~1 in 5 children has dyslexia; most aren't identified until ~3rd grade. Families face long waitlists, ~$2,000 evaluations, and $75–$100/session tutoring. High urgency, high trust-sensitivity.
- **Differentiation (the moat) — the "trust hierarchy":**
  1. **Specialist-first honesty** — openly says a specialist is best and helps you find one; Phono is the *bridge*, not the replacement.
  2. **Parent, not professor** — built by a parent of dyslexic kids who did the research.
  3. **The science, said simply** — rigorous method in plain language.
  4. **Built for struggling readers from the ground up.**
  5. **"Start tonight"** — every tool is one doable action this evening.
- **Stage:** pre-launch. Fluency toolkit content is complete; Summer toolkit launches next, timed to summer-slide urgency.
- **Go-to-market:** organic short-form social → email list → toolkit sales, with a free reading-check screener as the lead magnet. Success is measured by saves, shares, email signups, and email→sale conversion — not follower count.
- **Pricing:** not finalized (see open questions), but always positioned as *dramatically* cheaper than tutoring, with action-oriented language ("Get the Kit," "Start Tonight").
- **Year-1 goals:** roughly $25k revenue and ~1,000 Summer toolkit sales; lean, AI-assisted, mostly solo operation.

---

## 4. The goals (for the redesign)

A redesign should make the product:
1. **Feel trustworthy and credible** to a scared parent, without sounding clinical or salesy — reinforcing the honest, specialist-first, parent-to-parent voice.
2. **Lower the barrier to starting** — make "start tonight" feel true: clear, calm, low-friction, obviously doable by a non-expert.
3. **Protect the child's confidence** — everything child-facing must be low-pressure, encouraging, shame-free, and success-celebrating.
4. **Make progress and consistency visible** in a motivating, non-judgmental way (effort and time, not accuracy/comparison).
5. **Scale across media** — work in print today and extend cleanly to web and an app later, and across grade-banded products.
6. **Reinforce, not dilute, evidence credibility** (structured literacy / Orton-Gillingham / IDA alignment).

---

## 5. The constraints (objective only)

- **Accessibility is core, not optional.** Child-facing reading content must be maximally legible and easy to decode; audio/read-aloud support matters; feedback must never shame; progress shown by effort, not accuracy; low cognitive load and low time-pressure throughout. Validate against a recognized standard (e.g., WCAG).
- **Two reading levels:** plain language for adults; carefully decodable content for children — handled distinctly.
- **Media:** print is the current primary medium (must print cleanly at home on standard equipment); responsive web and a mobile-first app are future phases.
- **Minimal materials:** sessions should rely on common household items only.
- **Compliance:** future child-facing software triggers children's-privacy obligations (verifiable parental consent, data protection); content is proprietary (IP must be assigned by any contributor); avoid medical/diagnostic claims; keep standards-alignment honest.
- **Localization:** English/US-only for now; don't foreclose future internationalization.

## 6. What is deliberately NOT in this brief

No prior logo, color palette, typography, layout, component styling, design system, illustration, icon set, or any earlier UI/visual decision is described anywhere in this folder. That omission is intentional: the agency should design the visual identity fresh, guided only by the customer, product, business, goals, and constraints above.

## 7. Start-here summary

> Phono helps a frightened parent of a struggling or dyslexic reader (K–8) **start helping their child tonight**, using the same evidence-based, structured-literacy methods a specialist would use — translated into short, doable, shame-free home sessions, and honestly pointing them toward a real specialist as the next step. The product is a set of grade-banded toolkits (print today; chatbot, app, and specialist directory ahead). It wins on **trust, honesty, simplicity, and accessibility**. Design for a calm, credible, encouraging experience that an untrained adult can deliver and an anxious child can enjoy.
