# 06 — Business Context

Market, competitors, positioning, differentiation, and goals. No visual branding.

## Market

- **Size of need:** ~1 in 5 children has dyslexia; most aren't identified until ~3rd grade. Dyslexia represents the large majority of learning disabilities. Untreated reading difficulty is a leading driver of school struggle.
- **Customer economics:** Parents face long evaluation waitlists (cited 9–14 months), private evaluations around $2,000, specialist tutoring at $75–$100/session, and commercial structured-literacy programs in the hundreds of dollars per month. There is high emotional urgency and high sensitivity to trust and cost.
- **Timing pressure:** Evidence points to the window before age 8 as most important, which raises the cost of waiting and creates urgency.
- **Geography:** US-first (founder is in North Carolina). International is undecided; assume mostly-US initially.

## Competitors / alternatives

- **Specialist tutors** ($75–$100/session) — the gold standard, but expensive and waitlisted.
- **Commercial OG / structured-literacy programs** — comprehensive but costly, complex, and time-intensive for families.
- **Generic reading apps** — accessible but typically not dyslexia-specific or evidence-aligned.
- **School intervention** — slow to start and inconsistent in quality/availability.
- **Doing nothing / waiting** — the default the product is fighting against ("wait and see").

Market research on direct-sell marketplaces suggested existing competition is weak and poorly marketed — read as an open opportunity.

## Positioning

**Working positioning statement:**
> "Phono gives parents of struggling and dyslexic readers the evidence-based tools to start helping their child tonight — built on the same science specialists use, written for the parent at the kitchen table."

**Movement framing:**
> "The end of 'wait and see.' A movement of parents who refuse to lose another year, who learn the science the system didn't teach them, and who help their kids now. Anti-guessing. Pro-evidence. Parent-powered."

## Differentiation (the trust hierarchy — the core moat)

1. **Specialist-first honesty** — openly says a trained specialist is best and helps the parent find one; positions Phono as the bridge, not the replacement. Counter-intuitive, and the strongest trust driver.
2. **Parent, not professor** — built by a parent of dyslexic children who did the research; credible *because* it mirrors the customer.
3. **The science, said simply** — Orton-Gillingham rigor in plain, neighbor-over-the-fence language.
4. **Built for struggling readers from the ground up** — not a generic product with an accessibility mode added on.
5. **"Start tonight"** — every tool resolves to one doable action this evening; no long ramp-up or required training.

## Business model

A layered model, described as concentric circles:
- **Core:** the toolkits (evidence-based, downloadable, grade-banded). Current revenue.
- **Platform:** website, parent chatbot, community, and an interactive session app.
- **Network:** a directory of vetted, certified specialists for referral/partnership — fulfilling the specialist-first promise.

**Revenue streams (current and envisioned):**
- One-time toolkit sales (current).
- Possible subscription/membership (full library + new content + community).
- Specialist directory listing fees / featured listings (future).
- Possible affiliate partnerships and paid live workshops (future).

> **Pricing is not yet locked** and differs across documents — treat as unresolved (see `08-open-questions.md`). Two pricing frames appear in the source material:
> - A lean direct-sales frame: ~**$24** Summer toolkit, ~**$37** Fluency toolkit, single purchases, no subscription — consistently framed against "$75–100 for one tutoring session."
> - An earlier tiered frame: a starter single-band toolkit (~$29), a complete library (~$69), and a monthly plan (~$12/mo).
>
> What's consistent: **price is positioned as dramatically cheaper than tutoring/clinical programs**, and purchase language is action-oriented ("Get the Kit," "Start Tonight," "Download the Kit") rather than transactional ("Buy Now," "Shop").

## Go-to-market

- **Phase / status:** Pre-launch (planning). The Fluency toolkit content is complete; the Summer toolkit is the near-term launch, timed for summer-slide urgency.
- **Primary channels:** short-form social (discovery/reach) + a second social channel (trust, saves, email capture), funneling into **email** as the main monetization path. A free reading-check screener is the lead magnet.
- **Content strategy:** organized around jobs-to-be-done content pillars — myth-busting (reach), the science said simply (authority/saves), honest/emotional posts (trust/shares), and tactical "do this tonight" demos (saves/product gateway), plus advocacy/proof content.
- **No paid ads** assumed in the base plan; growth is earned via shares/saves and collaborations with educators/specialists.
- **Metrics that matter:** saves-per-view, shares-per-view, email signups per post, and email→sale conversion — explicitly prioritized over follower count.

## Goals

- **Year 1 floor:** validate ~$25k revenue (target also stated as a $10k–$25k range).
- **Year 1 stretch:** ~1,000 Summer toolkit sales; email list of ~3,000–5,000.
- **Audience trajectory:** grow a genuinely-aligned following over 12 months; larger breakout reach treated as upside, not the base case.
- **Operating model:** stay lean and solo as long as possible, using AI agents across business functions; first hire envisioned as an editor/VA to scale content.

## Founder context (relevant to working style, not branding)

The founder is a parent of dyslexic children, building toward an "AI business builder" identity (directing AI to build products rather than coding traditionally). Works best with encouragement, clear next steps, and focus on what moves the needle. This favors **clarity, low-friction workflows, and strong defaults** in anything built for them to operate.
