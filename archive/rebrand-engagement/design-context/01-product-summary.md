# 01 — Product Summary

## What the product is

**Phono** is a company that produces **structured-literacy reading toolkits** for parents who are helping a child who struggles with reading — most often a child with dyslexia or suspected dyslexia.

- **Phono** is the company and mission.
- **Toolkits** are the product the customer buys.

A toolkit is a self-contained, parent-led reading-intervention program. Each toolkit contains **20 "tools"** (individual instructional activities/resources) organized into **4 skill units**. A parent runs short sessions with their child using these tools. No special training is required of the parent; every tool includes step-by-step parent guidance ("what to say, what to do, what to do when the child makes an error").

The product line is grade-banded:

| Toolkit | Grade band | Scope | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundation** | K–2 | Full program | Planned |
| **Fluency** | Grades 3–5 | ~30 weeks / ~60 sessions, 20 tools | **Built (all 20 tools complete)** |
| **Independence** | Grades 6–8 | Full program | Planned |
| **Summer** | Cross-grade (K–8, dual track) | ~10 weeks / 20 sessions | In active research & planning |

The first products are delivered as printable documents (a downloadable bundle of printable activity files, plus a welcome packet and reference materials). Later phases envision software: a parent chatbot, an interactive session app, and an AI tutoring agent (see Section "Long-term vision" and `06-business-context.md`).

## Why it exists

Roughly **1 in 5 children** has dyslexia, and most are not identified until around 3rd grade. When a parent realizes their child is struggling, they hit a wall:

- Formal school evaluations can take **months** (waitlists cited at 9–14 months).
- Private evaluations can cost **~$2,000**.
- Specialist tutoring runs **$75–$100 per session**; commercial structured-literacy programs can run **hundreds of dollars per month**.
- Meanwhile, the research says the **window before age 8 matters most**, and "wait and see" costs time that is hard to recover.

The result is a gap: a parent knows something is wrong and wants to help **tonight**, but trained help is months and thousands of dollars away. Phono exists to fill that gap.

## What problem it solves

Phono lets a parent **start helping their child immediately**, using the **same evidence-based methods a specialist would use**, translated into plain language and short, doable sessions — while they wait for, search for, or work alongside a professional.

The product explicitly does **not** position itself as a replacement for a specialist. Its stated stance: a trained specialist is the best outcome; Phono is the bridge that keeps the child moving forward in the meantime.

It solves three concrete problems for the parent:
1. **Access/time** — something effective to do right now, instead of waiting months.
2. **Confidence/competence** — a way to run sessions correctly without being trained, so they aren't "just guessing."
3. **Cost** — an affordable on-ramp versus per-session tutoring or expensive programs.

For the child, it solves the underlying reading problem with **explicit, systematic, multisensory** instruction, while protecting the child's confidence and reducing the shame and avoidance that commonly surround reading.

## Who benefits

- **Primary beneficiary / buyer:** A parent of a struggling or dyslexic reader (K–8) doing reading support at home. (Detailed in `02-customer-personas.md`.)
- **End user of the instruction:** The child (K–8) who struggles with matching letters to sounds and reads slowly and effortfully.
- **Amplifiers (not the buyer):** Classroom teachers, reading interventionists, and speech-language pathologists aligned with the science of reading — they lend credibility and may share or reference the materials.

## Primary use cases

1. **"Start tonight" home intervention** — a parent runs a short (15–20 minute) structured session with their child, several times a week.
2. **Summer maintenance** — preventing reading regression ("summer slide") during the school break with a lighter cross-grade program.
3. **Bridge while waiting** — productive structured practice during the months-long wait for an evaluation or specialist.
4. **Self-assessment / triage** — a parent uses a free reading-check screener to decide whether to watch, look closer, or act now (see `04-features.md`).
5. **Reference for educators** — teachers/SLPs use materials in their own practice and as a credibility signal.

## Long-term vision

Two layered visions are stated in the source material:

**Product vision** — move from printable toolkits toward a connected platform:
- **Phase 1–2:** Toolkit content + a website and automated sales.
- **Phase 3:** A **parent-guide chatbot** that answers parents' questions 24/7, trained on the toolkit content and able to escalate complex cases.
- **Phase 4:** An **AI dyslexia tutoring agent** that works directly with children.
- **Phase 5:** An **interactive session app** that guides a parent through sessions, tracks progress, and adapts.
- **Ongoing:** A **specialist directory** connecting families to vetted, certified tutors — the "network" layer that fulfills the specialist-first promise.

**Founder/company vision** — build a lean business where every function (operations, support, marketing, finance, legal) is powered by AI agents; the founder's goal is to become an "AI business builder" who directs AI to build products rather than coding traditionally.

The mission statement that anchors all of this:

> "We stand in the gap between struggling families and trained specialists — giving parents the tools, guidance, and support to help their child right now, while connecting them to the experts who can take them further."
